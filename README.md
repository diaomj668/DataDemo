# Global Energy: Cost, Supply & Exposure

An interactive executive briefing that compares energy demand, business electricity prices, generation mix, and international energy trade across 15 countries. Built entirely from the supplied `energy-data.csv` dataset.

[Open the briefing](https://global-energy-executive-briefing.diaomj668.chatgpt.site/) · [Explore the source data](dist/energy-data.csv)

The hosted OpenAI Sites deployment is private and may require owner access. Access to this GitHub repository does not grant access to the hosted site.

## Features

- Four executive findings calculated from the dataset.
- Six interactive charts with country highlighting, tooltips, and applicable sorting controls.
- Country profiles showing prices, consumption, domestic production, generation mix, and trade.
- Comparison mode for up to four countries, initially China, United States, India, and Germany.
- Searchable data table, full CSV download, and a reset control.
- Expandable methodology and country-specific source links.
- Responsive layouts and keyboard-accessible controls.

## The six charts

| Chart | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Energy demand | Total national energy consumption, in exajoules (EJ) |
| Business electricity prices | Retail business electricity prices, in USD/kWh, with local-currency details |
| Electricity generation mix | Domestic generation shares by energy source |
| Primary energy consumption mix | Primary energy consumption shares using substitution accounting |
| Energy trade exposure | Net energy imports, with exporters to the left and importers to the right |
| Electricity cost and fossil share | Business electricity price versus fossil generation share; bubble area represents domestic electricity generation in TWh |

Select a country to spotlight it throughout the briefing. Change **Chart scope** to **Comparison countries** to focus the charts and table on the selected comparison group.

## Run locally

This is a static website using HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript. No package installation or build step is required. With Git and Python 3 installed:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/diaomj668/DataDemo.git
cd DataDemo
python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory dist
```

Open [http://localhost:8000](http://localhost:8000) in your browser. Stop the server with `Ctrl+C`.

## Data coverage and interpretation

The supplied snapshot contains 15 countries. It is not a complete world dataset or a time series.

| Measure | Reporting period in this dataset |
| --- | --- |
| Total energy consumption | 2024 |
| Electricity and primary energy mixes | 2024 |
| Business and household electricity prices | December 2025 |
| Total energy production and trade | 2023 |
| Electricity generation and trade volumes | 2024 |
| Source access date | September 16, 2026 |

Important distinctions:

- Business electricity rates are not specific industrial tariffs or total energy costs. The stated business usage basis is 1,000,000 kWh/year; consult individual source notes.
- Electricity shares describe domestic generation, not capacity or electricity consumption.
- Primary energy includes uses such as transport and heating. Its substitution accounting differs from the energy production and trade balances; do not multiply consumption shares by production totals.
- “Other” includes nuclear and other renewables and must not be treated entirely as renewable.
- Net imports equal gross imports minus gross exports. Negative net imports indicate net exports, not proof of energy independence.
- Missing values remain unavailable. Iran's missing business electricity price excludes it from the scatter plot.
- Fossil generation share is the sum of coal, gas, and oil/other fossil shares. It is not emissions intensity, and the scatter plot does not establish causation.
- Rankings describe the supplied sample. No unweighted country-average price is presented as a global average.

## Sources

The CSV contains original source URLs, definitions, reporting years, and notes for each country. These include GlobalPetrolPrices, Worldometer, Our World in Data, the International Energy Agency, and the U.S. Energy Information Administration. The site reproduces the supplied observations without fetching live data.

Open **Methodology & sources → Country records & original source links** in the briefing, or inspect the CSV's source URL columns, to trace individual records. Review original providers' terms before redistributing their data.

## Validation

The initial implementation was checked for:

- All six charts rendering without browser JavaScript errors.
- Default four-country comparison, selection limit, sorting, and table search.
- Missing-price handling and desktop/mobile layout, including horizontal overflow.
- Energy trade reconciliation and electricity generation shares summing to 100% in the supplied records.

The optional browser WebMCP comparison tool is feature-detected. Native WebMCP validation was unavailable in the browser used for these checks; normal website controls work without it.
